<h4>🎶 How to Practice and Prepare for Your Recital</h4>

<p>Our Fall Recital is coming up soon, and we couldn’t be more excited to hear all of our amazing students perform! Whether it’s your first time on stage or your tenth, preparation is the key to feeling confident and having fun on performance day. Here are a few tips to help you get ready!</p>

<h4>1. Listen, Listen, Listen</h4>

<p>The more you <em>listen</em> to your songs, the better they’ll sink in. Try to listen while you’re riding in the car, getting ready in the morning, or doing homework. You’ll start to naturally remember how each section feels — where it gets louder, softer, faster, or more intense. Knowing how the song <em>should</em> sound will make your playing smoother and more expressive.</p>

  <h4>2. Play With the Track</h4>

<p>Practicing along with the original recording or a backing track helps you stay in time and feel the energy of the song. It also shows you where tricky transitions or rhythm changes might happen. If you’re in Rock Shop, this is a great way to make sure you’re locking in with the band.</p>

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/Guitar_Lessons_Wylie_Fall_2024-114.jpeg" alt="" srcset="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/Guitar_Lessons_Wylie_Fall_2024-114.jpeg?format=100w 100w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/Guitar_Lessons_Wylie_Fall_2024-114.jpeg?format=300w 300w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/Guitar_Lessons_Wylie_Fall_2024-114.jpeg?format=500w 500w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/Guitar_Lessons_Wylie_Fall_2024-114.jpeg?format=750w 750w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/Guitar_Lessons_Wylie_Fall_2024-114.jpeg?format=1000w 1000w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/Guitar_Lessons_Wylie_Fall_2024-114.jpeg?format=1500w 1500w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/Guitar_Lessons_Wylie_Fall_2024-114.jpeg?format=2500w 2500w" width="640" height="427" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

  <h4>3. Focus on the Tough Spots</h4>

<p>Don’t always start from the beginning! Isolate the section that gives you trouble — maybe a fast lick, a tricky chord change, or a spot where you forget the rhythm. Loop it, slow it down, and repeat it until it feels easy. Small, focused practice sessions are way more effective than running through the song over and over.</p>

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/BD155F30-8A5D-4CA7-B3D7-06CCCDEB9B03.jpeg" alt="" srcset="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/BD155F30-8A5D-4CA7-B3D7-06CCCDEB9B03.jpeg?format=100w 100w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/BD155F30-8A5D-4CA7-B3D7-06CCCDEB9B03.jpeg?format=300w 300w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/BD155F30-8A5D-4CA7-B3D7-06CCCDEB9B03.jpeg?format=500w 500w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/BD155F30-8A5D-4CA7-B3D7-06CCCDEB9B03.jpeg?format=750w 750w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/BD155F30-8A5D-4CA7-B3D7-06CCCDEB9B03.jpeg?format=1000w 1000w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/BD155F30-8A5D-4CA7-B3D7-06CCCDEB9B03.jpeg?format=1500w 1500w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/BD155F30-8A5D-4CA7-B3D7-06CCCDEB9B03.jpeg?format=2500w 2500w" width="4500" height="3000" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

  <h4>4. Memorize the Structure</h4>

<p>By now, you should know the structure of your song — verse, chorus, bridge, solo, outro. Try playing the song without looking at your notes or chord chart. If you get lost, that’s a clue about what needs extra attention. Confidence grows when you <em>know</em> where you’re going next.</p>

  <h4>5. Perform at Home</h4>

<p>A great way to practice for the stage is to <em>simulate</em> the performance. Play for your family, your dog, or even a stuffed animal audience! This helps you get used to the feeling of playing in front of people. If you mess up, just keep going — that’s what professionals do.</p>

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/B903CA46-8E07-4AC8-8379-6E54266EEF5C.jpg" alt="" srcset="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/B903CA46-8E07-4AC8-8379-6E54266EEF5C.jpg?format=100w 100w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/B903CA46-8E07-4AC8-8379-6E54266EEF5C.jpg?format=300w 300w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/B903CA46-8E07-4AC8-8379-6E54266EEF5C.jpg?format=500w 500w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/B903CA46-8E07-4AC8-8379-6E54266EEF5C.jpg?format=750w 750w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/B903CA46-8E07-4AC8-8379-6E54266EEF5C.jpg?format=1000w 1000w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/B903CA46-8E07-4AC8-8379-6E54266EEF5C.jpg?format=1500w 1500w, {{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/B903CA46-8E07-4AC8-8379-6E54266EEF5C.jpg?format=2500w 2500w" width="2500" height="1666" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

  <h4>7. Rest, Relax, and Enjoy It</h4>

<p>A recital isn’t just about perfection — it’s about <em>sharing your music</em>. Nerves are totally normal, but remember: everyone in the audience is cheering for you. Take a deep breath, smile, and have fun!</p>
